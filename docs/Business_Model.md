# ClubsHub one-page business model

**Product:** ClubsHub, a campus-wide club discovery and management website for the University of Eastern Africa, Baraton (UEAB).

## Customer and problem

**Primary users are students.** Students lack one place to find UEAB clubs and often hear about a club only when an event happens. That means they can miss opportunities to join. Club administrators also need a simple way to reach prospective students between events. We interviewed **12 students**. We did not record how many were first-years and make no first-year-specific claim.

## Solution and value

ClubsHub puts club discovery in one searchable directory. Students can explore clubs, personalize discovery, request membership, track request status, and browse events and announcements. Club administrators manage profiles, membership requests, members, announcements, and events. Students can also propose clubs. Students can see available communities before deciding where to participate, instead of relying on chance event discovery or visiting separate club sites.

## Costs and adoption

The app is hosted for free at this stage. A school hosting plan has not been agreed, and no cost estimate has been produced. The team plans to discuss hosting with the university once there is interest. To work toward the first 100 users, the team will begin with club leaders and members in its own department; the head of department has agreed to discuss that club. Adoption is expected to grow one club at a time. This is a starting approach, not a measured adoption forecast.

## Who pays

The team expects the university to pay because ClubsHub serves the campus as a whole. This is an expectation only: payment has not been confirmed by the university or the head of department.

## Risks and compliance

- **Separate club websites:** individual club sites could fragment discovery. ClubsHub's response is a single directory for campus clubs.
- **Stale listings:** students need current club information. Clubs that do not maintain their pages or post updates can leave the directory out of date.
- **Privacy and access:** the system stores student names, university emails, and membership request details. Role-based server access limits students to their own requests, club administrators to their club's requests, and super administrators to system-wide records. The project does not claim a formal compliance certification; access and data collection should continue to be reviewed as the service grows.
- **Prior attempt:** a departmental hackathon previously produced a website for the department's club that was not taken forward. ClubsHub targets the broader campus, while recognizing that this precedent makes sustained club participation a risk.
